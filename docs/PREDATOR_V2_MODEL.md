# Predator-v2: current 2D experiment

The default `/` route and retained `/predator.html` route run `dist/sim/predator-world.js`. The preserved 3D planet is at `/planet.html`. `legacy-v1` in `dist/engine.js` retains its exact trajectory fixtures. Predator-v2 changes the scheduling, sensing, motion, feeding, and controller schemas from the earlier predator increment; predator-v1 states are incompatible. Predator-v2 state version 2 checkpoints are upgraded on restore by adding a neutral goal-input weight to each first layer.

## Purpose, entities and units

The experiment tests how local food, predation, inherited traits and controller size interact. Length is in abstract LU, time in simulation seconds, and food/reserves/tissue in EU. Health and stamina are abstract point scales. These coefficients are design choices, not calibrated physiology.

The default world is 1000 × 700 LU with reflecting boundaries, 80 prey, 8 hunters and 230 finite food particles. Width and height can each be 250–4000 LU; boundaries can reflect or wrap periodically. Founder counts are absolute, so resizing changes initial density. Food arrives externally. Organisms, food and carcasses have increasing IDs. The renderer uses a world-matched canvas and uniform cover scale with pan, zoom, follow and inverse click mapping; camera state is excluded from model state.

Organisms store position, velocity, heading, inherited speed/sense/size, pigment, markings and neural weights, reserve and tissue energy, gut content, health, stamina, age, role, parent ID, actions and cooldowns. Role is a diet/physiology template, not a species claim. Body size, tail length and feelers render the inherited size, speed and sensing traits; pigment and markings are cosmetic inherited genes. Predators keep a warm triangular silhouette and prey a green-to-teal oval silhouette. Cosmetic founder variation and mutation use deterministic ID/seed draws without consuming ecological RNG streams. Death creates one finite carcass containing remaining reserve, gut and tissue energy. Predator carcasses become edible to hungry predators. The last 50 death summaries are retained.

## Scheduling and interventions

The engine advances exactly 1/30 simulation second per tick. Controllers run every six ticks. Browser playback requests whole ticks at 1×/3×/8×, executes at most eight per animation frame, and spends at most about 12 ms per frame on a batch after its first tick. The pending queue is capped at 30 ticks to keep controls responsive under overload. The page shows selected and achieved speed, plus a device-limit label when demand is dropped. A long background gap pauses playback. Rendering and inspection consume no model RNG.

Commands have an integer target tick and sequence number. Renewal, mutation, predator introduction and Skyfall apply in sequence at the start of that tick. The two sliders and Skyfall queue a next-tick change; predator introduction uses an explicit future tick. Skyfall chooses a seeded interior impact point and affects living organisms within 32% of the shorter world dimension. Casualties become ordinary carcasses, so their stored energy remains in the accounting ledger and can be scavenged. The impact ring is visible for 45 ticks, and the latest casualty count is shown below the controls. Introduced predator reserve and tissue are booked as external energy. Scalar command changes record previous values. RNG states for initialization, environment, mutation, brain initialization and rule search are separate and included in exported state.

`stepTick` updates in place when the next tick cannot reach an organism, food or carcass technical budget. When a budget could be reached, it stages a model-state copy; a rejected tick then leaves state and RNG unchanged, and the page pauses and labels the run censored. Raising the hit budget resumes the same next tick. These budgets are not ecological carrying capacities. Near-budget ticks still pay the cost of copying a large neural genome.

Tick order: commands → food arrival and carcass decay → age/starvation checks → local sensing and due decisions → accelerated motion and costs → simultaneous contact damage → surviving-organism feeding → digestion, stamina recovery and repair → reproduction → history. Newborns enter after interactions. The technical-limit rollback guarantee applies to `stepTick`, not to isolated direct system calls used by fixtures.

The Decision lab pauses the live run and copies its complete checkpoint twice. One branch keeps current food arrival; the other queues a 20-percentage-point change (downward if the current value exceeds 75%). Both advance for 300 ticks using the same initial seed and state. The panel reports final prey, predator and food counts, a short interpretation based on prey survival, and controls to apply the tested food level or continue unchanged. This is a single seeded 10-second comparison, not a population forecast or a claim of statistical certainty. The live simulation state is not changed during comparison.

## Space, sensing and controllers

A 64 LU uniform grid narrows local queries; exact minimum-image distances follow it. Periodic queries cover seams even when world dimensions are not cell-size multiples. Reflecting boundaries reverse velocity. Desired velocity follows bounded turn and throttle outputs; actual velocity approaches it at at most 180 LU/s². Optional static circular obstacles block line-of-sight and attacks. Founders and offspring require unoccupied body space, and swept movement stops at another living body or obstacle, including across periodic seams. A tick-varying ID priority resolves moving-body claims independently of storage order. Crowded births wait without spending energy. A configurable obstacle editor is still absent.

The shared `predator-observation-v3` schema has 23 ordered channels in `dist/sim/predator-neural.js`: local food/threat direction and detection masks, reserve, health, walls, oscillator, gut, stamina, attack readiness, radial target velocity, maturity, previous action, apparent target size and Goal turn. The new node is the bounded turn toward a visible edible target, away from a visible predator for prey, inward from a close wall, or toward a stable exploration heading when no target is visible. Threat escape takes priority over food pursuit. Exploration headings change every 300 ticks and come from the organism's ID and seed, without consuming ecological RNG. Missing targets have zero direction/velocity/size and a zero mask. Configurable field of view is 30–360°; default 360° means all-around sight. Visible records include position, motion, size and declared trophic role, without genes or brain data. Role recognition is perfect. Circular obstacles occlude targets; noise and fixed sensory sectors remain pending.

Rule and neural controllers use the same observation and are limited to 3 rad/s turn and throttle 0…1. The rule controller searches, follows visible food, flees visible threats and requests feeding/reproduction. Neural outputs are turn, throttle, attack, feed and reproduce. For neural organisms, Goal turn contributes 60–85% of the applied turn according to the immediate goal; neural turn supplies the remainder. A 40% minimum throttle supports exploration and pursuit. Visible edible goals request feeding, and visible prey goals request an attack, while physical reach, reserve, stamina, maturity and cooldown still govern outcomes. This is explicit survival guidance for initially random networks, not lifetime training; evolved weights still affect turning, faster movement, reproduction and behavior outside those reflexes. The ensemble uses equal averaging. Count 0–4, hidden depth 1–3 and width 2–12 are independent restart settings. One width-6 hidden layer has 179 parameters; four three-layer width-12 networks have 2,660. Controller cost uses each individual's parameter count. Normal inference omits activation traces; the selected trace is reconstructed from its last input without changing model state.

## Physiology, reproduction and accounting

Prey eat food particles; hunters prefer living prey and prey carcasses. When a hunter's reserve plus assimilable gut content falls below 35% of capacity, it can also hunt another predator or eat a predator carcass. Prey and carcasses remain the preferred targets. Cannibal kills appear in the population metrics. Finite bites enter bounded guts. Digestion rates are 45 EU/s for prey and 60 EU/s for hunters; assimilation efficiencies are 0.75 and 0.8. The rest dissipates as heat. Tick-varying ID priorities arbitrate contested pools without double consumption. Default hunter bite limit is 60 EU/s; prey can bite up to 29 EU in a tick. Undigested food can remain in a gut when reserve is full.

Eligible hunter attacks require contact, positive intent, at least 2 EU reserve, 10 stamina and an expired 15-tick cooldown. They spend 2 EU and 10 stamina. Damage is `max(0, 30 - prey armor)` health points, aggregated before lethal results. Kills award no instant energy. Stamina recovers at 12 points/s for 0.05 EU per point; health repairs at 1 point/s for 0.3 EU per point. The heat ledger separates attack, repair, stamina, basal, locomotion, sensing, controller, reproduction and digestion costs. Refuge and handling-time mechanics remain pending.

An adult aged at least 10 seconds, with sufficient reserve, positive reproduction intent and expired cooldown can bud. The parent loses 90 EU: child reserve 55 EU, tissue 32 EU and heat 3 EU. Offspring inherit role and independently copied/mutated traits, pigment, markings and weights. Tissue nutrient investment and development stages are not modeled, so births satisfy an energy identity but not the planned nutrient identity.

`energyResidual()` compares current organism reserve + gut + tissue + food + carcass energy to initial endowment + external input − dissipated heat. The isolated 10 EU feeding fixture yields 7.5 EU reserve gain and 2.5 EU heat at 75% assimilation. There is no limiting-nutrient ledger, producer growth, detritus pool or closed recycling; food renewal is an external subsidy.

## Evidence and open gates

`npm test` covers 18,000-tick one-founder render schedules, command order, technical rollback/resume, grid queries against brute force, periodic seams, camera inversion, living-body exclusion through reproducing runs, crowded birth deferral, field-of-view masks, hand-set neural response, contested digestion, armor/stamina, combat, extinction, accounting and legacy trajectories. A Safari visual check of the live 2D route confirmed that the simulation fills its canvas without side bars at desktop width. The VS Code integrated browser previously showed a selected hunter's reserve, gut, health and stamina. Narrow-width visual validation remains open.

The headless benchmark is [PREDATOR_V2_BENCHMARK.json](PREDATOR_V2_BENCHMARK.json): Node v23.11.0 on Apple M3 Pro took about 0.21 s for 300 default rule ticks and 0.14 s with one two-layer width-12 neural controller, both above the 240 ticks/s needed for 8× model time. Browser FPS was not measured. P01–P05 acceptance gates remain open for complete energy and nutrient accounts, cohort diagnostics, a disclosed coexistence/extinction seed set, richer sensory sectors and larger workload performance.
